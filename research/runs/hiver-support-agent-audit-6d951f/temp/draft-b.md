## 1. Problem Framing — What Good and Trustworthy Mean for One Brand

This submission picks one brand from the Customer Support on Twitter corpus and builds an agent that does three things: assigns each inbound customer tweet to one of a small set of intents, drafts a reply grounded in how that brand actually resolved similar cases, and decides auto-handle versus escalate with a stated reason. The proof matters more than the pipeline. Good therefore means narrowly scoped, measurable, and auditable — not large, clever, or complete.

The contrarian thesis of this draft is simple: at take-home scale — one brand, a subsample the evaluators will actually run, a 15-minute reproduction budget, and a 150–250-item golden set — the simplest architecture that clears the trust bar wins, and most of the complexity the field recommends is premature. A native-LLM-first classifier with retrieval-filtered few-shot prompts, dense-only retrieval over thread windows, a hosted evaluation suite, and a thin escalation gate will beat a SetFit-first plus hybrid-plus-rerank plus code-first-harness stack on trust per engineering hour, unless brand-data measurements compel the upgrade. That claim is falsifiable, and this draft names the exact measurements that would falsify it.

What "good" means depends on the brand. The corpus-wide exploratory work shows AmazonHelp at roughly 169,840 tweets and 168,823 responses with median response around 11.47 minutes, AppleSupport at roughly 106,860 tweets and 106,648 responses with median around 70.97 minutes, and TMobileHelp with median response near 2.75 minutes versus British Airways near 180.5 minutes [[github-vishesh062customer-support-tweet-classifier-multi-class-text-classifier-f]] — figures consistent with the per-brand exploratory counts surfaced in the depth work on brand selection. A fast-response telecom brand with short threads rewards auto-handling of status and how-to intents; a slow-response airline or device brand with long multi-turn troubleshooting punishes premature auto-handling and rewards escalation precision. The brand choice should therefore maximize submission quality — volume sufficient for 200 golden threads, response coverage sufficient for grounding, thread depth sufficient for escalation signal, and an issue space that compresses to about seven distinguishable intents — not raw row count. AmazonHelp is the default recommendation on volume and response density; TMobileHelp is the contrarian alternate where fast, templated resolutions make auto-handle defensible. Either is defensible; what is not defensible is picking the largest brand without checking resolution consistency.

Trustworthy means four independently measured properties. Intent routing is correct often enough that the wrong department does not see the ticket. Replies are grounded: every actionable claim is entailed by cited brand history, not by the model's parametric memory. Escalation is calibrated: the auto-handled slice has bounded risk and every escalation carries a reason code a handler can act on. And the numbers themselves are honest: splits are conversation-disjoint and forward-looking, the judge's agreement with humans is reported with uncertainty, and the headline is accompanied by the section the assignment mandates on what is misleading about it.

Deliberately not built: no multi-agent orchestration, no learned sparse retrieval, no fine-tuned generator, no 77-label taxonomy, no real-time streaming evaluation, no production PII vault beyond redaction. Each exclusion is a bet that the complexity buys less trust than the same hours spent on golden-set adjudication and leakage control. The rest of this draft defends those bets one by one and concedes where the measurements forbid them.

## 2. Data Foundation — Conversation Graph, Brand Selection, Splits, and Leakage Control

The raw schema of thoughtvector/customer-support-on-twitter supports full thread reconstruction from tweet identifiers, author identifiers, inbound flags, timestamps, and reply linkage [[interim-leakage-safe-temporal-split-vs-random-stratified-split]]. The TNE-AI conversation derivative preserves that structure explicitly at 794k rows across 109 companies [[interim-leakage-safe-temporal-split-vs-random-stratified-split]], while the 945k input-output-pair derivative destroys it by flattening threads into isolated pairs [[interim-leakage-safe-temporal-split-vs-random-stratified-split]]. That distinction is load-bearing: any step that flattens threads to pairs silently removes the grouping key the split must respect.

Three leakage mechanisms are real on this data. Same-thread overlap means consecutive turns share entities, phrasing, and resolution state, so a classifier or retriever that saw turn k-1 already knows turn k. Retrieval contamination is directional: the knowledge base is brand outbound tweets, so it contains the gold answer to many evaluation queries, and fetching the query's own thread response makes groundedness look near-perfect. Future-answering-past means policy, outage status, and links drift across the collection window, so training on later tweets to answer earlier ones is temporal leakage as defined in the standard taxonomy that requires test data to postdate training data and splits to respect dependency structure [[interim-leakage-safe-temporal-split-vs-random-stratified-split]].

The forensic audit of the existing workspace is damning and must be stated plainly. The headline Apple inbound pool carries no thread identifier and no timestamp, so conversation-disjoint or temporal splitting is impossible from that artifact. The 200-row golden file carries text plus labels but no tweet identifier, thread identifier, or timestamp, so provenance is untraceable and overlap with few-shot or retrieval pools is unauditable by construction. All 200 golden texts match pool texts verbatim, confirming the golden set was drawn from the same population that feeds training and retrieval. The knowledge base is entirely outbound with tweet identifiers preserved, which structurally means it holds the brand's gold responses while golden queries are inbound from the same brand threads — and because neither artifact carries thread identifiers, the highest-value contamination of fetching the exact gold answer cannot even be checked. A separate in-repo parquet file already carries thread identifiers, timestamps, positions, and a gap split, proving the thread-preserving recipe exists but was not applied to the headline pipeline [[interim-leakage-safe-temporal-split-vs-random-stratified-split]]. Current headline numbers are therefore upper bounds, not deployment estimates.

Here the contrarian must steelman the cheap side before conceding. Random tweet-level stratified sampling is faster, preserves label balance, needs no thread reconstruction, and suffices if the claim is explicitly scoped to interpolation on a static snapshot. Group-aware splitting on thread identifiers via the standard group-shuffle mechanism, where test and train fractions refer to groups rather than samples [[groupshufflesplit-scikit-learn-191-documentation]], plus time-ordered splitting where training sets are supersets of the past and test is the next fold with a gap parameter to exclude boundary-adjacent samples [[timeseriessplit-scikit-learn-191-documentation]], costs real engineering: rebuilding linkage from the raw file, connected components over the reply graph, near-duplicate collapse, temporal cutoff selection, and fail-closed overlap assertions on every harness run. For a one-week take-home, that cost deserves scrutiny.

It does not survive scrutiny. The standard leakage taxonomy defines both temporal leakage and non-independence between train and test as design-phase errors that produce wildly overoptimistic conclusions across hundreds of surveyed papers [[interim-leakage-safe-temporal-split-vs-random-stratified-split]], and the connectome replication study measures the inflation directly: leakage via repeated subjects drastically inflates prediction performance, with small datasets exacerbating the effect [[interim-leakage-safe-temporal-split-vs-random-stratified-split]]. Same-thread tweets are repeated subjects; a 150–250-item golden set is a small dataset. Both risk multipliers are present simultaneously. Selective-prediction work across 17 datasets further shows that in-distribution to out-of-distribution degradation is the norm rather than the exception [[interim-leakage-safe-temporal-split-vs-random-stratified-split]]. A random split answers how well the system interpolates the snapshot; the assignment asks whether the agent is good enough to trust on future messages. Those are different claims, and only the leakage-safe recipe licenses the second.

The recommended recipe is therefore non-negotiable despite its cost. Rebuild the brand slice from the raw file preserving tweet, author, inbound, timestamp, text, and both reply-linkage columns. Reconstruct threads as connected components over the reply graph with position and size metadata. Collapse near-duplicates via normalized-text match plus a similarity threshold so template replies do not spray across splits. Sort threads by start time, place knowledge base plus classifier training plus few-shot examples strictly before cutoff T1, and sample the 150–250 golden threads strictly after T1 with no straddling thread. Partition everything else with group-aware splitting on thread identifier with a fail-closed zero-overlap assertion. Build the knowledge base from pre-cutoff outbound only and exclude same-thread documents at evaluation time, reporting scores with and without the exclusion as a leakage diagnostic. Carry tweet, thread, and timestamp provenance on every artifact. Stratify threads, not tweets, within the post-cutoff window by weak-intent bucket, escalation flag, and thread-size bucket, with one disclosed backfill rule for rare intents unfillable after the cutoff. This is more work than random sampling. It is the difference between a number and evidence.

## 3. Intent Taxonomy — Deriving a Small Routable Measurable Label Scheme

The assignment asks for a small set of intents defined from the data. The majority position in this research program holds that 6–8 data-derived intents plus an explicit out-of-scope label is over-determined: model behavior and annotation arithmetic independently squeeze the taxonomy to that size. This draft agrees with the size but dissents on how to get there and what to borrow.

Start with what Banking77 actually is. It comprises 13,083 banking queries across 77 intents with 10,003 training and 3,080 test examples averaging roughly 59.5 and 54.2 characters respectively, clean single-domain banking English [[polyaibanking77-datasets-at-hugging-face]]. It exists to test fine-grained single-domain intent detection as a complement to coarser multi-domain sets [[polyaibanking77-datasets-at-hugging-face]]. It is explicitly not Twitter: no handles, no thread context, no sarcasm, no 280-character truncation, no outage spikes. Its legitimate use under the assignment rules is intent work only, never inflation of headline results. The strongest legitimate uses are prototyping the classifier harness, calibrating annotation guidelines on clean data before facing noise, and demonstrating that the pipeline handles fine-grained label spaces — all reported separately from brand numbers.

The case for a native-LLM-first classifier on the brand taxonomy is stronger than the SetFit-first majority admits. Controlled Banking77 work shows GPT-4 at 80.4 micro-F1 one-shot and 83.1 three-shot against a sentence-encoder baseline at 57.4 and 76.7 respectively, with full-data fine-tuning at 91.2 versus 94.1 [[interim-setfit-vs-llm-intent-with-uncertainty-routing]]. More importantly for this assignment, in-context learning with large models beats fine-tuned non-generative models with fewer examples and handles wording variance that dominates Twitter [[230814634-breaking-the-bank-with-chatgpt-few-shot-text-classification-for-financ]]. Zero training, no backbone surgery, no contrastive pipeline — a prompt with representative demonstrations per intent plus an explicit out-of-scope label. For fewer than roughly five examples per class, the empirical rule favors the language model; fine-tuning wins above it [[interim-setfit-vs-llm-intent-with-uncertainty-routing]]. A take-home golden set with 200 threads across seven intents sits near that boundary, and the rare intents that most need help sit below it. That is exactly where the native model earns its keep.

The SetFit-first counter-evidence is real and must be engaged, not dismissed. The hybrid study most load-bearing for the majority shows a tuned sentence-encoder baseline at 0.600 average F1 rising to 0.658 with cheap negative augmentation, still roughly 8 points behind the best language model, with the encoder running roughly 56 times faster, and a variance-routed hybrid landing within about 2 percent of native-model F1 at roughly half the latency [[interim-setfit-vs-llm-intent-with-uncertainty-routing]]. SetFit's published design dispenses with prompts entirely via contrastive Siamese fine-tuning plus a classification head, training in half a minute on a single GPU where a multi-billion-parameter alternative needs an order of magnitude more [[setfit-efficient-few-shot-learning-without-prompts]]. Cost-cascade work shows learned routing across models matching the best single model at up to 98 percent cost reduction or improving accuracy at equal cost [[230505176-frugalgpt-how-to-use-large-language-models-while-reducing-cost-and-imp]]. And retrieval-filtered dynamic few-shot selection — retrieving the top 5–20 demonstrations by embedding similarity rather than stuffing hundreds into the prompt — lets a cheaper model at 85.5 percent beat a full-prompt frontier run at 83.1 percent while cutting cost from roughly 740 to 42 dollars per 3k queries on Banking77 [[interim-setfit-vs-llm-intent-with-uncertainty-routing]].

The steelman-contrarian resolution is sequential, not tribal. Default to native-LLM-first with retrieval-filtered dynamic few-shot prompts for the submission, because it is the fastest path to a trustworthy number: no training infrastructure, robust to wording variance, handles rare intents with fewer than five examples, and the prompt-engineering cost is hours rather than days. Contest it against a SetFit challenger only if time permits, and promote the challenger only if it beats the native baseline on the brand golden set at fixed routing budget with uncertainty bars that exclude noise. The mandatory baseline the router must beat is temperature-scaled maximum softmax probability, because the broadest selective-prediction comparison finds nothing consistently beating it across in-distribution, out-of-distribution, and adversarial settings [[interim-setfit-vs-llm-intent-with-uncertainty-routing]], while efficient single-pass softmax is competitive with multi-pass dropout estimates at far lower cost on text classification [[221014037-revisiting-softmax-for-uncertainty-approximation-in-text-classificatio]]. If the challenger cannot beat that floor on area under the risk-coverage curve across both in-distribution and temporal splits, it does not ship — a contest the majority position endorses in principle but rarely enforces in practice.

Two constraints bind both architectures. First, the backbone must match the domain if any encoder is trained: Twitter-pretrained models outperform generic encoders on noisy tweet classification, and generic paraphrase encoders leave measurable performance on the table [[fine-tuning-language-models-on-dutch-protest-event-tweets-acl-anthology]]. A SetFit challenger built on a generic paraphrase checkpoint without a Twitter-pretrained ablation has not been fairly tested. Second, synthetic augmentation for rare intents must be capped. Language-model-generated training data boosts classifiers when intents are sufficiently distinct but generates neighbor-intent utterances when intents are close [[data-augmentation-for-intent-classification-with-off-the-shelf-large-language-mo]], with gains turning negative past roughly seven synthetic examples per intent and real data always beating synthetic [[interim-setfit-vs-llm-intent-with-uncertainty-routing]]. The brand taxonomy will contain close pairs — delayed versus lost versus damaged, billing versus refund — which is the worst case for synthetic bleed. Permit at most a tiny synthetic ablation at seven or fewer per intent, labeled ESTIMATED until golden-gated, and never as the primary fix for rare-intent starvation.

Taxonomy derivation itself should be data-driven but cheap. Cluster a few thousand inbound brand tweets with a Twitter-pretrained embedding, inspect clusters manually, draft 6–8 candidate intents with one-line scope definitions plus out-of-scope, pilot-label 30 items with two annotators, and merge any pair exceeding roughly 15 percent cross-confusion. The precedent of seven routing categories over 1.5 million inbound tweets with a TF-IDF baseline at 98.2 percent accuracy against a fine-tuned transformer at 99.5 percent [[github-vishesh062customer-support-tweet-classifier-multi-class-text-classifier-f]] is both encouraging and cautionary: encouraging because small taxonomies route well, cautionary because keyword-derived labels at that scale are a keyword detector with extra steps and the dominant fallback bucket distorts every metric. Human adjudication on the golden 200 is what separates a routing scheme from a keyword list.

## 4. System Architecture — Minimal Design That Maximizes Trust per Complexity

The minimal trustworthy system has five stages and no framework. Ingest rebuilds threads with provenance. Classify assigns intent with a calibrated confidence. Retrieve fetches similar resolved threads. Generate drafts a grounded reply with citations. Decide gates auto-handle versus escalate with a reason code. Every stage logs its inputs, outputs, and latency. The whole pipeline runs from a frozen subsample with pinned dependencies and reproduces headline numbers in under 15 minutes.

The framework question deserves its most honest hearing here because the majority position dismisses frameworks too quickly. LangChain reached stable 1.0 in late 2025 with over 126,000 stars and more than 500 integrations [[rag-vs-langchain-which-do-you-need]], and its graph extension handles stateful multi-step workflows with conditional routing that plain scripts reimplement badly. The programmatic alternative that compiles declarative model calls into self-improving pipelines has substantial community adoption at tens of thousands of stars [[github-stanfordnlpdspy-dspy-the-framework-for-programmingnot-promptinglanguage-m]]. For agentic retrieval — where the model grades retrieved context, loops back for more, or falls back to search — graph-based orchestration improves complex query handling substantially in production traces [[rag-vs-langchain-which-do-you-need]]. Dismissing that entire apparatus as bloat is the majority's weakest move.

It is still the right move for this submission. The pipeline above is a linear retrieve-then-generate loop with one conditional branch at the escalation gate. Practitioner consensus across multiple independent sources holds that plain Python with direct provider APIs is faster to build, debug, and maintain for simple retrieval pipelines, with cleaner tracebacks and no abstraction layers hiding the actual call [[is-langchain-becoming-too-complexbloated-for-simple-rag-applications-in-2025-com]]. The measured framework overheads — roughly 10ms per query for the general orchestrator versus 6ms for the retrieval-specialized one and near-zero for plain code [[rag-vs-langchain-which-do-you-need]] — are small in absolute terms but compound with token overhead at scale, and the version-churn history of the graph layer has forced rewrites on teams that adopted early. The decision rule experienced developers actually use is dispositive: if the pipeline fits in under roughly 100 lines with direct calls, it does not need the framework [[rag-vs-langchain-which-do-you-need]]. This one does. Log the graph-framework path as a considered rejection with the reversal condition — adopt it if and only if the escalation policy grows a genuine loop with re-retrieval, grading, and fallback — rather than as ignorance of its existence.

Storage follows the same minimalism. At one-brand subsample scale of tens to hundreds of thousands of chunks, every candidate store answers queries in single-digit to tens of milliseconds [[chromadb-vs-qdrant-vs-faiss-vs-pgvector-vector-database-for-local-rag-local-llmn]], and the broader comparison concurs that the performance differences only bite at larger scales [[vector-databases-for-rag-faiss-vs-chromadb-vs-pinecone-vs-qdrant-pythonbookio]]. The vendor-neutral benchmark harness covering seven engines across million-scale datasets confirms that recall-latency tradeoffs are tunable everywhere and that the engineering differences at this scale are operational, not decisive [[github-mohitnagpaldcevector-db-benchmark-vendor-neutral-benchmark-of-7-vector-da]]. An in-process index plus a lexical index in the same process — FAISS or Chroma for vectors, rank-bm25 for terms — reproduces in minutes with no Docker, no server, and no credentials. A unified database with native hybrid ergonomics is the documented scale-up path, not the submission default. Data engineering uses Polars plus DuckDB over Parquet rather than pandas over CSV, on the standard 10–100x speedup argument at multi-million-row scale, with the raw file checksummed and the subsample frozen.

Security and privacy are thin but explicit. Redact handles, phone numbers, order identifiers, and URLs before prompts leave the process; wrap graded content as untrusted with injection guards; never echo personal identifiers into replies; pin secrets in environment, never in notebooks. Support prompts otherwise leak, and the escalation paths for sensitive actions are required practice, not ornament.

## 5. Retrieval and Reply Generation — Grounding Replies in Historical Resolutions

Reply generation is retrieval-bound: unretrieved truth cannot be generated, and a fluent confident reply built on the wrong thread is the exact hallucination shape that kills trust. The majority position mandates hybrid lexical-plus-dense fusion with cross-encoder reranking over thread-window chunks as the default. This draft defends dense-only as the submission default and forces hybrid to earn its place — then concedes the condition under which it almost certainly will.

The dense-only case is stronger than hybrid advocates admit. At subsample scale a single embedded store with metadata filtering handles thread, timestamp, and role constraints natively, builds in one command, and serves under 30ms [[chromadb-vs-qdrant-vs-faiss-vs-pgvector-vector-database-for-local-rag-local-llmn]]. Naive rank-fusion without tuning adds only about a point of blended quality in the standard benchmark [[interim-hybrid-retrieval-payoff]], while the operational ledger is real: two indices to version and keep fresh against embedding drift, two serving paths to parallelize and timeout-budget, fusion edge cases around empty lists and depth-coupled normalization, per-stage attribution logging, and a new hyperparameter surface spanning retrieval depth, fusion constants, rerank count, and context size. For paraphrase-heavy support queries — "my internet is down" matching "service outage in your area" — dense generalization is exactly the right tool, and a well-chosen short-text encoder over thread windows with a one-line thread summary prepended may resolve the majority of traffic without any lexical arm.

The silent-failure mechanism is the counter-measurement that nearly sinks the dense-only default, and intellectual honesty requires stating it at full strength. A bi-encoder pools token representations into one vector, buying paraphrase tolerance while destroying the lexical identity of rare strings: error codes, order and tracking numbers, model variants, version strings, and flight numbers collapse into generic neighborhoods [[interim-hybrid-retrieval-payoff]]. Twitter support text concentrates every dense-hostile feature in under 280 characters — handles, hashtags, case numbers, version strings, URLs, abbreviations — and finance-domain benchmarks show lexical methods beating state-of-the-art dense encoders on identifier-heavy documents [[interim-hybrid-retrieval-payoff]]. The failure is silent: dense retrieval still returns something, the generator writes a fluent confident reply with wrong specifics, and no error fires. Evaluation sets built from accepted production answers systematically exclude these cases, so blended recall can look acceptable while identifier-stratum recall sits near 40 percent [[interim-hybrid-retrieval-payoff]]. That is not a theoretical degradation. It is wrong order numbers in customer-facing replies.

The stacked numbers for the full hybrid pipeline are correspondingly strong. Controlled enterprise measurement moves recall at 10 from 0.67 dense-only to 0.78 with fusion and 0.84 with reranking [[interim-hybrid-retrieval-payoff]]. Tuned hybrid with field boosting adds over 7 points where naive fusion adds barely 1 [[interim-hybrid-retrieval-payoff]]. The contextual variant that prepends chunk-specific context before both embedding and lexical indexing cuts failures by roughly half, with reranking cutting them by two-thirds [[interim-hybrid-retrieval-payoff]]. The lift concentrates almost entirely on exact-term queries at roughly 8–15 points [[interim-hybrid-retrieval-payoff]]. The single-store recipe for both arms in one SQL statement exists and is concrete [[how-to-build-hybrid-search-with-pgvector-and-bm25-in-postgres-ben-moataz]], and learned sparse expansion matches hybrid quality but multiplies query latency unless restricted to document-side expansion [[splade-vs-bm25-vs-dense-does-learned-sparse-retrieval-beat-hybrid-search]].

The contrarian synthesis is a gated default with a pre-registered reversal. Ship dense-only over thread-window chunks with metadata filtering as the submission baseline, and run the five-arm ablation — dense, lexical, naive fusion, tuned weighted fusion, plus rerank — stratified into identifier-bearing, paraphrase, and mixed queries with adversarial identifier probes drawn from corpus identifiers. Gate the hybrid claim on at least 5 points of identifier-stratum recall gain without blended regression and within latency budget, with rerank justified only if it moves top-rank precision while recall is flat. The honest forecast is that any brand with order numbers, tracking numbers, or device identifiers will clear that gate comfortably, at which point dense-only reverts to a documented baseline and hybrid ships. That concession is a strength of the design, not a defeat: the gate converts an architectural debate into a brand-data measurement the evaluators can rerun. Deliberately not built regardless: learned sparse third legs, per-query dynamic fusion weights, and agent-driven retrieval loops — each logged as a decision with its trigger condition.

Generation itself stays deliberately boring. One generator call, temperature near zero, thread-window context of at most 5–10 chunks, citations to retrieved thread identifiers required before any actionable claim, explicit refusal to invent policy, compensation, or timelines, and structured JSON output with reply, citations, and abstention flag. No fine-tuning of the generator: at golden-set scale there is no training signal that would not overfit, and prompt discipline plus retrieval quality dominate.

## 6. Escalation Policy — Automating Only What Is Safe to Automate

Escalation is the only component whose errors directly harm customers: a false auto-handle sends a wrong answer with the brand's voice, while a false escalation merely costs handler time. The policy must therefore be risk-targeted, reason-coded, and shift-aware, with coverage reported alongside every risk number.

The confidence doctrine defended here is unified and deliberately cheap. The primary signal is temperature-scaled maximum softmax probability from the intent head, fit on a held-out calibration slice. A learned calibrator combining confidence with a difficulty proxy is admitted only if it beats the scaled floor on both area under the risk-coverage curve and the ranking-isolated refinement metric across in-distribution and temporal held-out slices; otherwise the floor stands. That contest is mandatory because the broadest comparison finds no method consistently beating the simple baseline across settings despite extra data and compute [[interim-setfit-vs-llm-intent-with-uncertainty-routing]], and because monotone rescaling improves threshold interpretability without repairing ranking error — closing the curve requires better ordering, not better scaling [[interim-escalation-calibration-under-shift-depth-investigation]].

The reply stage needs a second, independent gate because intent confidence says nothing about grounding. A natural-language-inference entailment check of the drafted reply against retrieved brand exemplars, with a pre-registered threshold near 0.9, escalates as grounding failure regardless of intent confidence. Low entailment always escalates. That conjunction — confident intent and entailed reply — is the only path to auto-handle.

Threshold selection follows the risk-targeted procedure: sort calibration samples by confidence, sweep the threshold, trace the risk-coverage curve, and fix the operating point as the maximum coverage subject to risk at or below the target, with the recommended operating risk at 5 percent intent error on auto-handled traffic plus sensitivity reporting at 1 and 10 percent [[interim-escalation-calibration-under-shift-depth-investigation]]. The calibration split must be conversation-disjoint and temporal — the most recent time window, never a random slice — with temperature fit on one half and threshold selection on the other, the threshold locked before the golden set is touched, and the calibration-to-test generalization gap reported explicitly. Any threshold re-tuning on golden invalidates the headline. Conformal-style coverage language is forbidden without an explicit exchangeability disclaimer, because brand drift, issue spikes, and noise violate the premise the guarantee requires.

Over-abstention is the dominant cost at low risk tolerance and must be priced, not hidden. State-of-the-art vision-language systems answer under 8 percent of inputs at 1 percent risk with vanilla confidence, learned selectors barely double that, and nearly all correct predictions can be abstained on at strict tolerance [[interim-escalation-calibration-under-shift-depth-investigation]]. The report must therefore quote risk-coverage pairs plus the effective-reliability score that prices abstention, the generalized area metric that penalizes low coverage, the fraction of dataset-correct answers actually answered, and the share of correct predictions needlessly abstained on — never risk alone. Operationally, a single post-abstention second chance precedes final escalation: one retrieval re-query with reranking or one paraphrase re-ask, accepted only if recovered confidence clears a second pre-registered bar, with recovery rate and marginal risk logged. That pattern lifts coverage substantially without accuracy loss in question-answering studies [[interim-escalation-calibration-under-shift-depth-investigation]] and converts abstention cost into a measured win.

Every escalation logs a reason code with signal values, drawn from a five-code taxonomy: low intent confidence, grounding failure, sensitive action or personal data, ambiguity or missing slot, and novelty or out-of-distribution distance. The handoff shows the reason plus evidence and suppresses the uncertain draft unless a handler explicitly requests it, because informing humans of deferral without surfacing the uncertain guess improves downstream accuracy while revealing the low-confidence guess degrades it [[interim-escalation-calibration-under-shift-depth-investigation]]. The draft-visible alternative is the honest efficiency counter — handlers may work faster with the draft in view and recovery-before-escalate converts abstention cost into wins — but the only human-factors evidence in the corpus favors defer-only, so the burden of proof sits with the draft-visible challenger on brand data.

Cheaper gates deserve their steelman. A fixed confidence cutoff tuned once, a keyword tripwire for refunds and account changes, and a length heuristic for multi-intent messages would ship in an afternoon and catch the bulk of unsafe cases. They fail exactly where trust lives: uncalibrated cutoffs drift under shift, keyword lists miss paraphrased sensitivity, and heuristics cannot produce the reason codes the assignment grades. Keep the tripwires as defense-in-depth behind the calibrated gate, never as the gate itself. Unmeasured quantities — the exact operating risk, the entailment threshold, per-class thresholds — are labeled ESTIMATED until fit on brand calibration data.

## 7. Evaluation Baselines and the LLM Judge — Proving It Works

The evaluation harness measures four layers separately: intent routing, retrieval relevance, reply quality, and escalation decisions. Blending them into one score hides every failure the assignment asks to surface.

Baselines are honest, not strawmen. The trivial baseline is majority-class intent plus a fixed templated reply plus always-escalate: it scores the floor that any system must clear and demonstrates that the harness runs end to end. The simple baseline is TF-IDF with balanced logistic regression for intent — the configuration that reaches 98.2 percent accuracy against 99.5 percent for a fine-tuned transformer on 1.5 million keyword-labeled tweets while training in minutes on CPU in under 2MB [[github-vishesh062customer-support-tweet-classifier-multi-class-text-classifier-f]] — plus BM25 retrieval plus direct language-model drafting without reranking. That simple baseline is genuinely strong on clean phrasing and interchangeable with the transformer on on-topic tweets [[github-vishesh062customer-support-tweet-classifier-multi-class-text-classifier-f]], which is precisely why beating it on hard strata — sarcasm, mixed intent, identifier-heavy queries — is informative. The candidate is native-LLM classification with retrieval-filtered few-shot prompts plus dense retrieval over thread windows plus the calibrated escalation gate. Intent metrics are accuracy, macro-F1, per-class precision-recall, and confusion matrices; retrieval metrics are recall, mean reciprocal rank, and normalized discounted gain at standard cutoffs reported per stratum; reply metrics come from the judge below; escalation metrics are the full risk-coverage curve with the abstention-pricing companions from the previous section.

The judge question is where this draft is most contrarian and most constrained. The majority position installs a caged pointwise judge with cross-family separation and statistical length control as the headline instrument behind tiered agreement gates. This draft agrees with the cage but argues the hosted-evaluation-suite half of the stack deserves equal billing — and that hand-rolled harness code is the most overrated component in the submission.

Start with what judges can actually do. Chain-of-thought plus form-filling evaluation with a frontier backbone reaches 0.514 Spearman correlation with humans on summarization, outperforming prior reference-free methods by a large margin [[g-eval-nlg-evaluation-using-gpt-4-with-better-human-alignment]]. Output-based judges reach Pearson near 0.81 and 0.68 on code tasks, approaching human parity in narrow domains. A mid-tier judge with the right debiasing portfolio reaches 71 percent agreement at roughly a tenth of a cent per evaluation, 15 times cheaper than the frontier [[interim-hybrid-retrieval-payoff]]. Those numbers license a judge as headline instrument — within a cage.

The cage is non-negotiable because the bias inventory is settled. Swapping answer order flips roughly a third of pairwise verdicts [[why-llm-judges-are-biased-position-self-preference]]. Style and formatting bias dominates at an order of magnitude above position bias in the only multi-family controlled measurement, with markdown preferred over plain text by judges at far higher rates than by humans [[caged-judge-protocol-for-grounded-replies-interim-locus-caged-judge]]. Self-preference is structural rather than vain: judges over-rate low-perplexity familiar text whether or not they wrote it, with the frontier bias measured near 0.52 on the equal-opportunity metric [[caged-judge-protocol-for-grounded-replies-interim-locus-caged-judge]], and the clean disentangling score that subtracts gold judgments from own-output scores confirms the effect survives quality controls [[beyond-the-surface-measuring-self-preference-in-llm-judgments-acl-anthology]]. Verbosity direction varies by judge rather than pointing uniformly upward, so it must be measured per judge rather than assumed [[caged-judge-protocol-for-grounded-replies-interim-locus-caged-judge]]. Reference-free generation metrics are formally one model grading another — test-time optimizable, biased toward self-similar outputs, at times biased against higher-quality human outputs — and belong as diagnostics, never as progress measures.

The resulting protocol: pointwise anchored scoring as the primary instrument, which kills position bias by construction; three 1–5 anchored dimensions — groundedness with an entailment cap, resolution-usefulness with a deflection cap, safety-honesty with an invention cap — each with few-shot anchors plus a separate categorical escalation verdict; evidence citation before scoring; anti-verbosity and anti-format clauses with formatting normalized pre-judge; graded content wrapped as untrusted; judge family disjoint from every generator family; agreement gates requiring human-human certification before any judge number is quoted and judge-human promotion bars with swap-consistency, length-score, and self-preference audits thereafter; system comparisons by paired significance tests with multiple-comparison correction and a pre-registered minimum detectable effect near 6–8 points at n=200; pairwise double-swap reserved strictly for close-call system comparison with tie-on-disagreement. Reference-free faithfulness scores run unanchored as diagnostics, preferably via a non-model entailment classifier to break judge-on-judge circularity [[faithfulness-ragas]].

Here the hosted-suite steelman lands. Versioned datasets plus an experiment runner plus regression errors plus CI integration are production reality in the leading open observability platform [[interim-hybrid-retrieval-payoff]], and the framework comparison literature converges on a two-layer pattern: a code-first framework for the offline gate paired with a tracing platform for production, with the most common 2026 setups pairing exactly those halves [[llm-evaluation-frameworks-compared-deepeval-vs-ragas-vs-langsmith-vs-braintrust]] [[best-llm-rag-evaluation-tools-in-2026-deepeval-vs-ragas-vs-langsmith-vs-phoenix]]. Code-first pytest-style evaluation with dozens of ready metrics and CI blocking [[llm-evaluation-frameworks-compared-deepeval-vs-ragas-vs-langsmith-vs-braintrust]], declarative YAML matrix testing with red-teaming for prompt comparison [[ai-evaluation-frameworks-ragas-deepeval-and-promptfoo-compared-2026-knovo]], and retrieval-specific metrics separating context precision and recall from faithfulness [[ai-evaluation-frameworks-ragas-deepeval-and-promptfoo-compared-2026-knovo]] each do work a hand-rolled script reimplements badly. The promptfoo-style declarative suite in particular earns its place for this assignment: cross-model prompt comparison plus injection and jailbreak probing in one command is the cheapest security evidence available [[intro-promptfoo]]. The majority's code-first-everything stance undervalues that the evaluators will trust a hosted experiment link with versioned datasets and regression history more than a notebook printout. Ship the harness as versioned code invoking hosted datasets and experiments, not as either alone — and never let reference-free scores headline, because the judge that grades its own homework cannot certify itself.

Golden-set certification gates the entire edifice. All 200 threads carry double or triple independent labels with confidence ratings; intent agreement must clear the reliability line for ground truth with per-label floors and prevalence-robust supplements where labels are rare; ordinal reply dimensions and the imbalanced escalation decision clear their own tier-appropriate bars; the sarcasm stratum is reported separately and never averaged away; disagreements route to a named adjudicator whose one-sentence rationale becomes a dated guideline changelog entry rather than a silent majority vote. The famous 80-percent aggregate agreement figure is context, never a pass criterion [[interim-golden-set-adjudication-and-agreement-gate]].

## 8. Failure Modes Headline-Number Critique and Limits

Five failure modes will dominate the error budget, each with a hypothesized mechanism and a disposition.

First, identifier hallucination: the retriever returns a same-brand wrong-thread resolution and the generator copies its order number, tracking number, or timeline into the reply. Expected behavior is abstention or a correctly cited specific; actual behavior is fluent wrong-specifics with citations to the wrong thread. Root cause is the dense silent-failure mechanism plus thread-window confounding. Mitigation is the hybrid gate from Section 5 plus the entailment gate from Section 6; shipped only if the ablation clears the gate, otherwise documented as the top residual risk.

Second, sarcasm and negation inversion: "love how you charge me for nothing" routes to praise or general instead of billing complaint, and the reply thanks the customer. The author-labeled sarcasm literature shows third-party agreement near 0.36–0.39 with nearly a third of sarcastic intent missed [[interim-golden-set-adjudication-and-agreement-gate]], so this stratum is expected to underperform every gate. Mitigation is the separately reported sarcasm slice plus a negation-aware prompt clause; the gate itself is not relaxed to accommodate it.

Third, multi-intent threads: a tweet about a technical fault ending in a billing mention fires the billing rule while the substance is technical — the exact confusion pattern dominating the baseline's error cells [[github-vishesh062customer-support-tweet-classifier-multi-class-text-classifier-f]]. Expected behavior is the primary-substance label with a secondary flag; actual behavior is single-label misfire. Mitigation is the ordered decision procedure in the labeling guideline plus an ambiguity reason code; multi-label output is a documented non-build.

Fourth, temporal staleness: a reply cites a pre-cutoff policy, link, or outage status that has since changed. Expected behavior is escalation on stale evidence; actual behavior is a grounded-in-the-past but wrong-today reply. Root cause is the knowledge base freezing at the cutoff. Mitigation is recency metadata in the prompt plus the novelty reason code; live policy lookup is a documented non-build.

Fifth, judge-rewarded verbosity: longer replies outscore concise correct ones, laundering wordiness into quality. Expected behavior is length-neutral scoring; actual behavior is a measurable length-score correlation. Root cause is the style and verbosity bias family. Mitigation is formatting normalization, anti-verbosity rubric clauses, and the length-controlled regression check; any headline that fails the length audit is withdrawn.

What is misleading about the headline number — the mandatory section — has four entries. The leakage entry states that any number measured before the split rebuild is an upper bound inflated by same-thread overlap and target-in-knowledge-base contamination, with the with-versus-without exclusion diagnostic quantifying the inflation on brand data (NOT MEASURED until the rebuild; ESTIMATED direction is upward bias). The imbalance entry states that the fallback general bucket's 45-percent share [[github-vishesh062customer-support-tweet-classifier-multi-class-text-classifier-f]] lets a predict-general default score misleadingly high accuracy while failing every class that matters, which is why macro-F1 and per-class tables headline instead. The judge entry states that the reported correlation is task-scoped and that self-preference is structural and irreducible by prompt instruction alone, with the cross-family separation and bias audits as the only transfer argument. The sample-size entry states that at n=200 every metric carries confidence bands spanning roughly 5–10 points, so any sub-6-point system gap is noise and every comparison prints its interval.

## 9. Reproducibility Security Scalability and One-Week Roadmap

Reproducibility is a graded deliverable disguised as infrastructure. The README reproduces headline results in under 15 minutes from a frozen subsample: one clone, one dependency install with pinned versions, one download of the raw file plus checksum, one command that rebuilds threads, splits, indices, golden pointers, and baselines, and one command that runs the harness and prints the headline table with confidence intervals. The subsample is a frozen Parquet snapshot with thread identifiers and timestamps intact, sized so the full loop fits the budget on a free-tier GPU or CPU-only machine with the transformer baseline optional. Random seeds are fixed and logged; the harness asserts zero thread overlap between golden and every training and retrieval artifact on every run and fails closed. The scorer implementations are versioned library calls where possible rather than bespoke code, on the replicability principle that hand-rolled scorers silently move numbers [[if-you-build-your-own-ner-scorer-non-replicable-results-will-come-acl-anthology]].

Security is thin but audited. Personally identifying information is redacted before any prompt leaves the process; graded content is wrapped as untrusted with injection guards; replies never echo identifiers; secrets live in environment variables with a committed example file containing only placeholders; the red-teaming suite probes injection and jailbreak behavior and its results ship in the appendix. The evaluation comparison literature singles out declarative red-teaming as the capability other frameworks largely ignore [[llm-evaluation-frameworks-compared-deepeval-vs-ragas-vs-langsmith-vs-braintrust]], which is why it runs even in the minimal configuration.

Scalability is documented as a path, not a pretense. First-stage retrieval latency is a non-issue at this scale with every store under tens of milliseconds below a million vectors [[chromadb-vs-qdrant-vs-faiss-vs-pgvector-vector-database-for-local-rag-local-llmn]]; the reranker dominates end-to-end latency and is capped at 50 candidates. Token and cost accounting reports dollars per thousand queries for each arm, with the retrieval-filtered few-shot pattern as the cost control that beats full-prompt frontier runs on both accuracy and price [[interim-setfit-vs-llm-intent-with-uncertainty-routing]]. Concurrency, index freshness, and embedding-drift monitoring are described as scale-up procedures with trigger thresholds, not implemented systems. The managed-store and unified-database migrations are named with their switching conditions.

The one-week roadmap spends days where trust is minted. Days 1–2: rebuild threads from raw, select the brand on the six measurable criteria, implement the leakage-safe split with provenance, and re-run the forensics as a passing assertion. Days 3–4: adjudicate the 200-thread golden set with double labeling, pilot-calibrated guidelines, and the tiered agreement gates; freeze it. Day 5: ship native-model-first classification with retrieval-filtered prompts, dense-only retrieval over thread windows, and the calibrated escalation gate with reason codes; run the trivial and TF-IDF baselines on identical splits. Day 6: run the three gated ablations — SetFit challenger versus probability floor, hybrid versus dense-only on identifier strata, hosted-suite judge versus human agreement — and promote challengers only on pre-registered wins. Day 7: write the six-page report with the misleading-headline section, the failure-mode table with real examples, and the decision log; record the demo. Anything that does not fit — learned sparse retrieval, agent loops, generator fine-tuning, multi-label taxonomy, live policy lookup — is cut explicitly with its trigger condition, not silently dropped.

## 10. Opinionated Synthesis — Committed Recommendations and Decision Log

Committed recommendation: submit a native-LLM-first, dense-only-baseline, hosted-suite-evaluated, thin-gated system on one high-density brand, with every complexity upgrade pre-registered behind a brand-data gate. AmazonHelp is the default brand on volume and response density; TMobileHelp is the approved alternate where fast templated resolutions favor auto-handling. The classifier is a native model with retrieval-filtered dynamic few-shot prompts and an explicit out-of-scope label, contested by a Twitter-backboned SetFit challenger that ships only on ranking-quality wins over the scaled probability floor. Retrieval is dense over thread-window chunks with metadata filtering, contested by hybrid fusion plus reranking that ships only on the 5-point identifier-stratum test it will probably pass. The judge is a caged pointwise rubric instrument with cross-family separation behind human-first agreement gates, executed through versioned hosted datasets and experiments with declarative red-teaming alongside. Escalation is scaled confidence ANDed with an entailment gate at a risk-targeted threshold fit on temporal calibration data, with reason-coded defer-only handoff and one measured recovery attempt. Splits are conversation-disjoint and forward-looking with provenance everywhere; every pre-rebuild number is an upper bound.

Decision log:

1. One brand only, AmazonHelp default — maximizes golden-set sufficiency and grounding density; TMobileHelp alternate pre-approved for fast-resolution auto-handle story.
2. Native-LLM-first classifier as default, SetFit as gated challenger — zero-training robustness to wording variance and rare-intent handling outweigh encoder latency savings until brand ablation proves parity.
3. Retrieval-filtered few-shot prompts (top 5–10) instead of full label-set prompts — beats full-prompt frontier runs on accuracy at roughly one-seventeenth the cost on the closest controlled analog.
4. Dense-only retrieval as submission baseline with hybrid gated on 5-point identifier-stratum gain — minimizes operational surface; hybrid expected to trigger the gate and ship as the measured upgrade.
5. In-process indices (vector plus lexical) over managed stores — every option serves subsample scale in milliseconds; reproducibility outranks scale features.
6. No agent framework; plain Python with direct APIs — pipeline fits the sub-100-line rule; graph orchestration adopted only if escalation grows a genuine grade-and-retry loop.
7. DSPy-style programmatic prompt optimization rejected for the submission — its compounding value accrues over iterations the one-week budget does not contain; prompts are versioned templates instead.
8. Taxonomy capped at 6–8 data-derived intents plus out-of-scope; Banking77 restricted to harness prototyping and guideline calibration — 77 clean banking labels are a counter-model for noisy Twitter routing, never headline evidence.
9. Synthetic augmentation capped at seven per intent as an ESTIMATED ablation only — generation bleeds into neighbor intents exactly on the close pairs this taxonomy contains.
10. Escalation as scaled confidence ANDed with entailment gate at 5 percent operating risk with 1 and 10 percent sensitivity — prices both intent error and ungrounded generation; threshold locked before golden is touched.
11. Defer-only handoff (reason plus evidence, draft suppressed) — the only human-factors evidence favors it; draft-visible promoted only on handler-accuracy measurement.
12. Caged pointwise judge with cross-family separation as headline instrument; reference-free scores as diagnostics only — correlations up to 0.81 are task-scoped while self-preference is structural.
13. Hosted versioned datasets plus experiment runner plus declarative red-teaming alongside code-first harness — evaluators trust versioned experiment links with regression history over notebook printouts.
14. Leakage-safe temporal group-aware splits with provenance and fail-closed assertions as Phase 0 — every pre-rebuild number is an upper bound; random stratified sampling rejected as the headline basis despite its speed.
15. Style-first bias portfolio (normalization plus anti-verbosity clauses plus length-controlled analysis) over swap-averaging as headline mitigation — controlled evidence ranks style an order of magnitude above position; swap retained as verification harness.
